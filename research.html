Nexus of geo-political risk, policy uncertainties and renewable energy production: The moderating roles of governance components
Akhter, T.* & Arif, M.A. · Energy Reports
Published Scopus Q1 WoS Q1

Abstract

This study investigates the impact of geopolitical risk, climate policy uncertainty, economic policy uncertainty, and energy-related uncertainty on renewable energy production in ASEAN and East Asian countries, while examining the moderating role of governance quality across six dimensions. Using a comprehensive panel dataset from 1990 to 2024, the analysis employs Fully Modified Ordinary Least Squares (FMOLS), panel Granger causality, nonlinear autoregressive distributed lag (NARDL), and structural break tests to capture long-run relationships, directional causality, asymmetric effects, and temporal shocks. The findings reveal that uncertainties, particularly economic policy uncertainty and geo-political risk, negatively affect renewable energy output, especially in weaker institutional environments. Governance dimensions such as control of corruption, government effectiveness, and political stability significantly moderate these impacts, while others governance indicators show limited or selective interaction effects. The NARDL results highlight asymmetric responses, where positive changes in uncertainty drive more pronounced impacts than negative shifts. Structural break analysis confirms the evolving nature of uncertainty effects during major global crises. The study underscores the importance of governance-sensitive and adaptive policy frameworks to shield clean energy productions from volatility and enhance energy resilience across the region.

Highlights

  • Governance reduces geopolitical risk and policy uncertainty impacts on renewable energy production.
  • Energy-related uncertainty is structurally resistant to governance that requires sector-specific stabilization measures.
  • The transitions to renewables in ASEAN and East Asian region depend on tailored institutional strategies.
  • Impacts of policy uncertainties on renewable energy exhibit strong asymmetric dynamics.
  • Effective renewable energy transitions require combined institutional and market-based instruments.

Keywords

Renewable energy production; Policy uncertainty; Geopolitical risk; Governance quality; ASEAN and East Asia; Asymmetric effects

Genuine or spurious asymmetry? Disentangling uncertainty effects on renewable energy consumption using Fourier-augmented panel NARDL
Arif, M.A.* & Furuoka, F. · Energy Nexus
Published Scopus Q1 WoS Q1

Abstract

A growing body of evidence documents asymmetric effects of uncertainty on renewable energy consumption, yet whether these asymmetries reflect genuine nonlinear responses or artefacts of unmodelled structural change remains unresolved. This study addresses that question using a balanced panel of 37 OECD countries over the period 1990 to 2024. The analysis employs a Pooled Mean Group Nonlinear ARDL framework augmented with trigonometric Fourier terms that capture smooth deterministic shifts without requiring prior knowledge of their number, timing, or functional form. Three uncertainty dimensions are examined simultaneously: climate policy uncertainty, energy-related uncertainty, and geopolitical risk. The results reveal that long-run asymmetry is genuine and variable-specific rather than uniform. Geopolitical risk displays the most pronounced asymmetry: adverse geopolitical shocks reduce renewable energy consumption substantially more than favourable shocks promote it. Climate policy uncertainty exhibits largely symmetric effects in the baseline model but undergoes a sign reversal for positive shocks once Fourier terms absorb smooth structural trends, demonstrating that uncontrolled structural change can mask underlying asymmetric patterns. Energy-related uncertainty shows the weakest asymmetric response. Long-run asymmetry is confirmed across specifications, establishing that the detected nonlinearities reflect genuine uncertainty-driven responses rather than unmodelled structural change. Short-run asymmetric effects are generally limited across all uncertainty dimensions, indicating that asymmetric transmission operates primarily through long-run adjustment channels. These findings underscore the importance of stable climate policy frameworks and geopolitical risk hedging strategies for sustaining the renewable energy transition in advanced economies.

Highlights

  • Fourier-augmented panel NARDL framework distinguishes genuine asymmetric uncertainty effects from smooth structural change.
  • Geopolitical risk shows strongest long-run asymmetric effect on renewable energy.
  • Climate policy uncertainty reverses sign under Fourier augmentation, revealing hidden asymmetry.
  • Energy-related uncertainty exerts weakest asymmetric effects.
  • Long-run asymmetry dominates; short-run effects limited, implying uncertainty shapes renewable transition through persistent channels.

Keywords

Renewable energy consumption; Climate policy uncertainty; Energy-related uncertainty; Geopolitical risk; Pooled mean group; Fourier NARDL model

Short Dynamic Panels with Cross-Sectional Average Instruments under Multifactor Errors
Arif, M.A. · SSRN Working Paper · Posted 26 Jun 2026
Working paper

Abstract

This paper proposes a generalised method of moments estimator for short dynamic panel data models in which the error structure contains unobserved common factors. The estimator combines the cross-sectional average device with internal instrumental variable estimation of the autoregressive parameter. The first-differenced lagged dependent variable is instrumented with lagged levels, which removes the bias that renders least squares estimation of the dynamic coefficient inconsistent under a fixed time dimension, while cross-sectional averages of the levels enter as controls that absorb the factor component. The estimator is consistent and asymptotically normal as the cross-section dimension diverges with the time dimension held fixed, and it does not require the number of factors to be specified. A simulation study shows that the estimator achieves the smallest median bias among competing procedures across a range of factor configurations, including settings in which the number of factors is unknown and in which the factor loadings are correlated with the regressors. The practical value of the estimator is illustrated with two cross-country growth applications. The first shows that accounting for the factor structure lowers the estimated persistence of output per capita. The second, an income convergence panel, shows that common global shocks explain much of the apparent persistence in cross-country relative income.

Highlights

  • Proposes a GMM estimator for short dynamic panels with unobserved common factors in the errors.
  • Instruments the differenced lagged dependent variable with lagged levels; cross-sectional averages absorb the factor structure.
  • Consistent and asymptotically normal as N→∞ with T fixed, without needing the number of factors specified.
  • Achieves the smallest median bias among competing estimators across a range of factor configurations in simulations.
  • Applied to cross-country growth data: the factor structure lowers estimated persistence and explains much of the apparent income-convergence persistence.

Keywords

Dynamic Panel Data; Cross-Sectional Dependence; Common Correlated Effects; Generalised Method of Moments; Fixed-T Asymptotics

Bias-Corrected Method of Moments Estimation for Short-T Dynamic Panel Data Models with Latent Group Structure
Arif, M.A. · SSRN Working Paper · Posted 28 Jun 2026
Working paper

Abstract

This paper proposes a bias-corrected method of moments estimator for short dynamic panel data models in which the slope coefficients vary across an unknown number of latent groups. Group membership is treated as an unobserved discrete classification, and within each group the slope coefficients are common. The estimator combines a within-group bias-corrected moment condition derived from the profile score of the Gaussian likelihood with a classification rule that minimises a quadratic form in the bias-corrected moments. The leading bias arising from the incidental parameters problem under fixed time dimension is removed within groups, and the classification step recovers the latent partition with probability approaching one as both the cross-sectional and the time dimensions grow, with the time dimension entering because classification of individual units requires the within-unit information to accumulate. The proposed estimator extends established split-panel jackknife bias correction methods to settings with discrete unobserved heterogeneity in the slopes, and it complements existing approaches based on quasi-differencing and the common correlated effects principle. Post-classification consistency and asymptotic normality of the group-specific coefficients are established under fixed time dimension and large cross-section, classification consistency is established as the time dimension grows, and a consistent rule for selecting the number of groups is provided.

Highlights

  • Bias-corrected method of moments estimator for short dynamic panels with an unknown number of latent slope groups.
  • Combines a within-group bias-corrected moment condition with a classification rule minimising a quadratic form in those moments.
  • Removes the incidental-parameters bias under a fixed time dimension while recovering the latent group partition.
  • Extends split-panel jackknife bias correction to settings with discrete unobserved slope heterogeneity.
  • Establishes post-classification consistency and asymptotic normality, plus a consistent rule for selecting the number of groups.

Keywords

Dynamic Panel Data; Bias Correction; Method of Moments; Latent Group Heterogeneity; Short Panels

Common Correlated Effects Estimation of Dynamic Three-Dimensional Heterogeneous Panel Data Models
Arif, M.A. · SSRN Working Paper · Posted 17 Jul 2026
Working paper

Abstract

Cross-section average-augmented panel regressions introduced by Pesaran (2006) have become a popular empirical tool for estimating panel data models with common factors, but the corresponding common correlated effects (CCE) framework has been developed only for static and two-dimensional panels. When bilateral data exhibit persistent dynamics and a hierarchical dependence structure in which global, origin-specific, and destination-specific factors operate simultaneously, neither static three-dimensional CCE estimators nor dynamic two-dimensional CCE estimators recover the short-run and long-run responses consistently. This paper shows that the CCE mean group approach extends to dynamic three-dimensional panels once each pair regression is augmented with current and lagged cross-section averages over three margins. Consistency and asymptotic normality follow from the joint expansion of both cross-sectional dimensions and the time dimension, with the convergence rate governed by the smaller dimension. Because the naive pair-dispersion variance estimator is inconsistent in this setting, a margin-based estimator is proposed and shown to be the only consistent choice. As standard plug-in estimators cannot account for all sources of estimation uncertainty, a cross-section bootstrap is suggested for inference on the persistence coefficient in moderate samples. An application to bilateral export flows recovers a small but precisely estimated idiosyncratic persistence once the hierarchical factor structure is absorbed, and Monte Carlo experiments confirm the theoretical results.

Highlights

  • Extends the common correlated effects (CCE) framework to dynamic three-dimensional (bilateral) panels with hierarchical factor structures.
  • Augments each pair regression with current and lagged cross-section averages over three margins to recover consistent short- and long-run responses.
  • Shows the naive pair-dispersion variance estimator is inconsistent and proposes a margin-based estimator as the only consistent choice.
  • Proposes a cross-section bootstrap for inference on the persistence coefficient in moderate samples.
  • Applied to bilateral export flows: recovers small, precisely estimated idiosyncratic persistence once the hierarchical factor structure is absorbed.

Keywords

Three-Dimensional Panels; Common Correlated Effects; Dynamic Heterogeneous Panels; Cross-Sectional Dependence
